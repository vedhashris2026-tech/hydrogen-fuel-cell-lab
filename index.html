Stage 1 · Hydrogen enters
Hydrogen reaches the anode catalyst and splits into protons and electrons.

Three steps, one reaction

A PEM fuel cell uses a membrane to separate charge and guide the reaction.

01 · Anode
H₂ → 2H⁺ + 2e⁻
02 · Membrane
Protons cross; electrons travel through the external circuit.
03 · Cathode
O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O
Why does electricity flow?
The membrane conducts protons but blocks electrons, so electrons travel through the external circuit and deliver energy to a load.

03 / Learn & explore

Think like a fuel-cell engineer

Connect the chemistry to real system-level performance.

⚛ Hydrogen · Fuel

Hydrogen supplies protons and electrons at the anode catalyst.

◉ Oxygen · Oxidant

Oxygen, usually drawn from air, reacts at the cathode.

⇄ Membrane · Separator

The PEM lets protons pass while directing electrons through the circuit.

♨ Output · Energy

The system delivers electricity while producing water and heat.

Experiment A · Increase stack size

Double the number of cells while holding voltage and current steady. Observe voltage and gross power.

Experiment B · Challenge the load

Raise the requested load above net output and watch load coverage.

Experiment C · Limit hydrogen feed

Reduce feed below the estimated need. The model flags the shortfall.

Experiment D · Account for auxiliaries

Increase power used by pumps and controls; compare gross and net output.

Model limitations & safety
This educational model assumes fixed average cell voltage and does not predict pressure, temperature, membrane hydration, transients or degradation. Hydrogen is highly flammable—this is a software demonstration, not instructions for building or operating a physical hydrogen system.
